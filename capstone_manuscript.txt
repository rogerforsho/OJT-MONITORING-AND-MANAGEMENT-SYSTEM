CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION
1.1  Background of the Study
In today’s fast-changing world, having academic knowledge alone is no longer enough to succeed in the workplace. Employers now look for individuals who not only understand concepts but can also apply them in real-life situations. This is where On-the-Job Training (OJT) becomes important, as it allows students to experience the actual work environment before they graduate. Globally, On-the-Job Training (OJT) is recognized as an important part of education that helps students transition from school to employment by bridging the gap between theoretical learning and practical application. Through exposure to real-world tasks and responsibilities, students develop not only technical skills but also essential soft skills such as communication, teamwork, adaptability, and time management. According to Garavan et al. (as cited in Bouchrika, 2026), OJT involves structured learning within organizations that improves job performance, while a study by Deloitte (2025) revealed that most Gen Z and Millennial workers consider soft skills essential for career growth. Globally, Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) are increasingly adopting Work-Integrated Learning (WIL) frameworks to enhance student employability and align academic curricula with evolving industry standards. Effective WIL programs depend on transparent monitoring, accurate verification of practical hours, and seamless collaboration between educational institutions and industry partners. In modern academic administration, digital transformation has shifted internship management away from disparate, paper-based tracking toward unified, cloud-hosted platforms. These automated platforms serve as central repositories for attendance logs, daily task reports, and performance assessments, ensuring data integrity, reducing administrative overhead, and maintaining compliance with institutional quality assurance standards.
In the Philippines, OJT is widely implemented in higher education institutions as part of the curriculum to ensure that students are prepared for employment. However, despite its importance, many challenges still exist in managing and monitoring OJT programs. Students often face problems such as lack of proper supervision, unclear assigned tasks, delayed feedback, and limited opportunities to apply their knowledge. At the same time, coordinators rely on traditional methods such as manual logbooks and paper-based reports, which are prone to errors, delays, and data inconsistencies. These issues make it difficult to effectively track student progress and ensure that internship requirements are met (Commission on Higher Education [CHED], 2021). A study conducted by researchers from Bohol Island State University titled “OJT AtTRACK – On-the-Job Trainee Remote Attendance Monitoring System using Face Recognition System” identified similar challenges in monitoring OJT programs, particularly in validating students’ attendance, tracking trainees’ progress, and managing records due to the heavy reliance on manual logbooks and paper-based monitoring. These traditional methods often resulted in inaccurate records, delayed reports, and possible attendance fraud. To address these issues, the researchers developed the OJT AtTRACK system, which utilizes face recognition and cloud-based technology for attendance monitoring. The system enables coordinators to monitor trainees in real time, automatically record attendance, compute rendered hours, and generate updated reports more efficiently. By replacing manual monitoring with a digital system, the study improved accuracy, transparency, and easier management of OJT programs (Bohol Island State University Researchers, 2023). Similarly, several studies highlight the importance of improving systems used in managing On-the-Job Training (OJT) programs. Cabalbag and Ontiveros (2024) developed a management information system that enhanced record tracking and improved data accessibility, while Reyes and Dela Cruz (2020) introduced an internship information system that reduced paperwork and increased data accuracy. These studies emphasize the importance of integrating digital solutions into OJT management to address inefficiencies and improve the monitoring process.
Currently, Colegio de Montalban still implements a manual process in handling the OJT requirements of fourth-year students under the ICS and IBE departments. The existing process involves distributing application forms, processing requests, submitting reports, monitoring student progress, and handling completion documents throughout the training period. Although the current system remains functional, it presents several challenges for the institution, faculty members, students, and partner companies. For professors and coordinators, manual management of student applications, attendance, progress reports, and related concerns requires significant time and effort, which may result in misplaced documents, delayed processing, inconsistent records, and difficulties in tracking students’ OJT status. These issues affect productivity and make it challenging for coordinators and professors to balance OJT responsibilities with their other academic tasks. Students also encounter difficulties in preparing and submitting physical requirements, as well as delays in communication regarding approvals, schedules, reports, and other OJT-related concerns. Since communication is conducted through different platforms and manual transactions, students may experience confusion, delayed responses, and difficulty monitoring their own progress and requirements. Similarly, partner companies face challenges in processing student applications, coordinating with the institution, monitoring trainee performance, and issuing completion certificates. Communication commonly relies on general-purpose applications such as Gmail, Viber, or Messenger, which are not specifically designed for OJT management. As a result, information may become scattered, difficult to organize, and less transparent among the school, students, and partner companies.
In response to these challenges, this study proposes the development of a Cross-Platform On-the-Job Training (OJT) Monitoring and Management System. The proposed system aims to provide a centralized and accessible platform for attendance monitoring, report tracking, performance evaluation, and communication among students, partner companies, coordinators, and supervisors involved in OJT programs. Through the use of modern technology, the system seeks to improve efficiency, accuracy, transparency, and the overall management of OJT processes. These challenges indicate that the current manual process is no longer efficient in addressing the increasing demands of OJT management. Therefore, the researchers propose the development of a Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System to address these concerns. The proposed system will serve as a centralized platform where schools, students, and partner companies can efficiently manage OJT-related processes. Specifically, the system aims to simplify attendance monitoring, report submission, application processing, progress tracking, and communication through digital automation and real-time monitoring. By implementing the proposed system, the researchers aim to reduce delays, minimize errors, improve transparency, and provide a more organized and effective OJT management process for the ICS and IBE departments of Colegio de Montalban.

1.2 Statement of the Problem
Currently, Colegio de Montalban manages the On-the-Job Training (OJT) operations for fourth-year students in the Institute of Computing Studies (ICS) and Institute of Business and Entrepreneurship (IBE) using fragmented and manual methods. Students encounter operational delays in submitting application requirements, tracking rendered internship hours, and communicating with program coordinators. Concurrently, faculty coordinators and partner company supervisors face administrative inefficiencies due to manual verification of attendance logbooks, delayed physical report submissions, and decentralized communication channels. These operational bottlenecks lead to inconsistent record-keeping, delayed progress tracking, and increased vulnerability to data errors. 
To address these challenges, this study covers the design, development, and implementation of a Cross-Platform On-the-Job Training (OJT) Monitoring and Management System. The proposed system provides centralized web and mobile platforms for real-time attendance verification, document management, progress tracking, and structured stakeholder communication. 
Specifically, this study seeks to answer the following sub-problems:
What are the current operational workflows, limitations, and administrative challenges in the existing manual OJT monitoring process at Colegio de Montalban regarding:
1.1 Attendance logging and rendered hour calculation? 
1.2 Requirement submission and document verification? 
1.3 Communication and performance evaluation between students, coordinators, and company supervisors? 
What features and architecture must be integrated into the Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System to address the identified operational limitations? 
What is the evaluation level of the developed system as assessed by IT experts, OJT coordinators, company supervisors, and student users based on the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Product Model in terms of:
3.1 Functional Suitability (Functional completeness, correctness, and appropriateness of application processing and progress tracking)? 
3.2 Performance Efficiency (Response time and data synchronization across web and mobile platforms)? 
3.3 Usability (User interface clarity, operational accessibility, and user satisfaction)? 
3.4 Security (Data privacy compliance, user authentication, and data integrity protection)?

 1.4 Conceptual Framework
 
Figure 1. Input-Process-Output (IPO) Model
This study adopts the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model to guide system development, helping organize and connect all its important parts (Ravitch & Riggan, 2016). It shows how different elements of the research relate to each other and helps researchers clearly understand and conduct a well-structured and meaningful study. This study follows the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model to guide the development of the Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System.
Input includes the current OJT process, identified problems such as attendance issues, delays in submission, inaccurate records, and lack of real-time updates. It also includes the respondent profiles composed of ICS and IBE students, OJT coordinators, and partner companies, as well as the user requirements and hardware and software requirements needed for the development of the proposed system.
Process involves the use of the Agile methodology in developing the proposed system. The process begins with the planning phase, followed by designing, developing, testing, deploying, and reviewing the system. These phases ensure continuous improvement, proper system functionality, and effective implementation of features necessary for OJT monitoring and management.
Output is the developed Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System. 
 
1.5 Operational Framework
 
Figure 2. Agile Methodology Development
The study will design and develop the Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System using the Agile Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) methodology. Agile is a software development approach that focuses on continuous improvement, collaboration, and step-by-step system development. This methodology allows the researchers to develop the system in smaller phases while continuously gathering feedback from users such as students, coordinators, and supervisors to ensure that the system meets their needs effectively (Kadenic, 2023).
The development of the system will begin with the planning phase, where the researchers will gather information regarding the current OJT process of the ICS and IBE departments of Colegio de Montalban. During this phase, interviews, observations, and consultations with students, professors, coordinators, and partner companies will be conducted to identify the common problems encountered in the manual OJT process. The researchers will determine the necessary features of the proposed system, such as attendance monitoring, report submission, progress tracking, notification management, and communication tools.
The next phase is the design phase, where the researchers will create the structure and interface design of the system. This includes designing the database, system architecture, user interfaces, navigation flow, and overall functionality of the application. Wireframes, flowcharts, and diagrams will also be prepared to visualize how users will interact with the system across different platforms.
After the design phase, the researchers will proceed to the development phase, where the actual coding and implementation of the system will take place. The researchers will develop the features identified during the planning phase, including login and account management, attendance monitoring, report uploading, progress tracking, notifications, and communication modules. Since Agile follows an iterative approach, the system will be developed gradually in smaller functional parts, allowing continuous improvements during development.
The fourth phase is the testing phase, where the developed system will be evaluated to identify errors, bugs, and technical issues. The researchers will conduct functionality testing, usability testing, and compatibility testing to ensure that the system operates properly across different devices and platforms. Selected students, coordinators, and supervisors will also test the system and provide feedback regarding its performance, accessibility, and ease of use.
The fifth phase is the deployment phase, where the developed system will be implemented and introduced to the intended users. The Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System will be utilized to improve attendance monitoring, report submission, communication, and progress tracking of students during their On-the-Job Training.
Lastly, the review phase will assess whether the proposed system successfully addresses the problems identified in the current manual OJT process. Feedback gathered from users during system implementation will be analyzed to determine the effectiveness, efficiency, accuracy, and usability of the system. Necessary improvements and revisions will then be implemented to further enhance the performance and functionality of the Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System.
 
1.6 Scope and Limitations
This study focuses on the design, development, and implementation of a Cross-Platform On-the-Job Training (OJT) Monitoring and Management System for Colegio de Montalban. It aims to centralize OJT processes and improve the efficiency, accuracy, and accessibility of training management.
Scope
The system covers the following functionalities:
·        Fourth-Year Students under ICS and IBE Departments – Use the system to record attendance through digital time in and time out, submit internship reports and requirements, upload documents, and monitor their OJT progress and rendered hours in real time.
·        OJT Coordinators and Program Heads – Monitor student attendance, validate submitted reports and requirements, review internship progress, evaluate student performance, and track completion of required internship hours.
·        Company Supervisors and HR Personnel – Verify student attendance, monitor trainee activities, and submit performance evaluations and feedback regarding students’ internship performance.
Limitations
The study is limited to BSIT 4th Year ICS and IBE students of Colegio de Montalban and excludes other academic levels and institutions. It does not cover job application processes, hiring systems, or MOA-related transactions. Administrative functions such as payroll and HR management are also outside its scope. The system requires a stable internet connection and does not support offline functionality. Furthermore, the development and evaluation of the proposed system are limited to the scope and duration of the study, which may limit long-term assessment and enhancement.

1.9 Definition of Terms
Attendance Monitoring – Refers to the process of recording and tracking students’ time-in and time-out during OJT using the developed system.
Camera-Based Selfie Verification – Refers to the system feature that captures a student’s image during attendance logging to verify identity and confirm actual presence.
Cross-Platform System – Refers to the developed system that can operate across multiple environments including web browser and mobile platforms.
Data Management System – Refers to the centralized digital system used to store, organize, and manage OJT-related records such as attendance, reports, and internship hours.
Functional Suitability – Refers to the extent to which the system provides functions that meet the requirements of OJT monitoring and management.
Internship Hours – Refers to the total number of hours rendered by students during their OJT as recorded and computed by the system.
Monitoring and Management System – Refers to the developed system designed to track, manage, and evaluate students’ OJT activities.
OJT Coordinator – Refers to the assigned school personnel responsible for supervising and managing students’ OJT activities.
Performance Evaluation – Refers to the assessment of student performance during OJT as provided by company supervisors.
Performance Efficiency – Refers to the system’s ability to perform tasks efficiently with minimal delay and optimal resource usage.
Progress Tracking – Refers to the system’s function of monitoring students’ completion of required OJT hours and requirements.
Reliability – Refers to the system’s ability to operate consistently and accurately without failure.
Report Submission – Refers to the process where students upload OJT-related reports such as daily, weekly, or monthly submissions through the system.
 Supervisor – Refers to the company personnel responsible for evaluating and monitoring student performance during OJT deployment.
System Evaluation – Refers to the assessment of the developed system based on ISO/IEC 25010 software quality standards.

The reviewed systems demonstrated how modern technologies such as face recognition, mobile applications, web-based platforms, cloud systems, and geolocation services can improve the efficiency and accuracy of internship monitoring processes.
The OJT AtTRACK system developed by Abad et al. (2023) uses face recognition and cloud technologies to automate attendance recording and trainee monitoring. The system provides real-time attendance validation, rendered hour computation, and automated report generation for OJT coordinators. The researchers emphasized that the use of facial recognition technologies minimizes attendance fraud and strengthens monitoring transparency. The system also improves the efficiency of monitoring students assigned to different internship locations by allowing coordinators to access updated trainee information remotely.
Similarly, the SEAIT OJT Monitoring System developed by Ramos et al. (2024) integrates face recognition technologies to improve attendance verification and accessibility. The system allows coordinators to manage attendance records digitally while improving monitoring transparency and reducing manual processes. The researchers highlighted that the integration of automated attendance verification helps reduce documentation workload and improve the reliability of attendance records. The study also emphasized that user-friendly systems improve usability for students, coordinators, and supervisors.
The Mobile-based Student Internship Monitoring System developed by Reyes et al. (2024) focuses on mobile accessibility and progress tracking. The system enables students and supervisors to monitor internship activities, attendance, and reports through mobile devices. The researchers explained that mobile-based technologies improve convenience and allow faster communication between institutions and trainees. The study also highlighted that progress tracking algorithms strengthen monitoring efficiency by allowing supervisors to track trainee performance and internship completion in real time.
The Web-Based Student Internship Monitoring Information System developed by Dewi and Hanifah (2024) improves internship attendance monitoring, report submission, and trainee documentation through a centralized online platform. The system allows institutions to monitor students remotely and improve communication between trainees and supervisors. The researchers explained that centralized web-based systems improve accessibility, strengthen documentation processes, and reduce the limitations commonly experienced in manual internship management systems.
Furthermore, the Android-Based Internship Attendance and Logbook Application Using Geofence Technology (2024) integrates geofence technologies into attendance monitoring systems. The system validates trainee attendance by verifying whether students are physically present within authorized internship locations before attendance can be recorded. The study highlighted that geofence technologies strengthen attendance authenticity and reduce opportunities for fraudulent attendance practices. In addition, digital logbook management improves the organization and submission of internship activities and reports.
The AttenFace system introduced by Rao (2022) uses real-time face recognition technologies to automate attendance verification and identity authentication. The study highlighted that intelligent attendance systems improve attendance reliability and strengthen monitoring security. The researchers also explained that real-time attendance monitoring systems reduce manual intervention and improve operational efficiency in managing attendance records.
Moreover, the Web-Based Internship Management System with Automated Attendance Tracking (2023) emphasized the importance of automation in managing attendance, trainee reports, and internship records. The system highlighted how automated attendance tracking and centralized databases improve documentation accuracy, monitoring transparency, and report generation processes.
The Cloud-Based Internship Monitoring System for Informatics Students (2025) also introduced the use of cloud technologies in improving internship monitoring and accessibility. The researchers explained that cloud-based systems allow users to access internship records and monitoring tools anytime and anywhere, making internship management more efficient and convenient for students, coordinators, and supervisors.
The existing systems demonstrated that the use of face recognition, mobile applications, cloud technologies, GPS, location-based verification, and automated monitoring features helps improve internship and attendance management. The studies highlighted how digital systems make monitoring faster, more accurate, and easier to manage through real-time tracking, centralized records, and automated attendance checking. These systems served as useful references for the proposed Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System because the proposed study also focuses on improving attendance monitoring, report submission, communication, progress tracking, and record management within one accessible and centralized platform.
Synthesis of Literature
Traditional internship and OJT monitoring processes commonly rely on manual attendance checking, paper-based documentation, physical report submission, and disconnected communication methods. These traditional processes often lead to delayed updates, inaccurate attendance records, poor communication, inefficient monitoring, misplaced files, and difficulties in handling large amounts of student information. Because of these challenges, many researchers focused on developing digital monitoring systems to improve efficiency, accessibility, transparency, and monitoring accuracy.
Many studies emphasized the importance of using technologies such as face recognition, artificial intelligence, deep learning, GPS, cloud systems, IoT, mobile applications, and web-based platforms in internship monitoring. Researchers explained that automated systems help improve attendance verification, support real-time monitoring, lessen manual tasks, and make monitoring processes more reliable. The studies also showed that centralized systems improve record management and make internship documentation easier to organize and access.
Studies related to face recognition and AI-based attendance systems emphasized that automated identity verification improves attendance accuracy and reduces fraudulent attendance practices. Researchers explained that integrating face recognition, computer vision, and deep learning technologies into attendance systems improves reliability, security, and operational efficiency. These systems also minimize human intervention and reduce the risks of inaccurate attendance records and attendance manipulation.
Meanwhile, web-based and mobile monitoring systems highlighted the importance of centralized databases, accessibility, and real-time communication in improving internship management processes. Researchers explained that web and mobile technologies allow students, coordinators, and supervisors to access monitoring systems conveniently regardless of location. The studies also emphasized that digital monitoring systems improve report submission, attendance recording, internship documentation, and trainee monitoring processes.
In addition, studies involving IoT and location-tracking technologies emphasized the importance of automated attendance validation and real-time monitoring in improving accountability and internship compliance. These technologies help verify trainee attendance, reduce fraudulent practices, and improve monitoring efficiency. Furthermore, cloud-based systems improve accessibility, centralized data storage, and real-time record management. 
Overall, the technology-based internship monitoring systems significantly improve efficiency, security, transparency, accessibility, communication, and monitoring accuracy. The studies consistently revealed that integrating intelligent technologies and centralized platforms strengthens internship monitoring processes while reducing the limitations commonly experienced in traditional manual systems. These findings strongly support the development of the proposed Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System for Colegio de Montalban because the proposed system also aims to automate attendance monitoring, improve communication, centralize trainee records, strengthen attendance verification, and improve overall internship management processes through digital and intelligent technologies.

Research Gap
Although existing studies and systems have improved internship and OJT monitoring through the use of face recognition, artificial intelligence, mobile technologies, web-based platforms, IoT, and cloud systems, several limitations remain in current monitoring and management platforms. Most existing systems focus only on specific functions such as attendance checking, report submission, or progress tracking, resulting in fragmented internship management processes.
Several related systems are limited to a single platform, either web-based or mobile-based, which reduces accessibility and flexibility for users across different devices. Some studies also focus primarily on attendance monitoring, without fully integrating important functions such as report management, internship time calculation, evaluation processes, communication tools, progress tracking, and notification systems into a centralized platform. Because of these limitations, users may still rely on multiple applications or manual processes to manage internship activities.
In addition, several systems implemented attendance verification using face recognition and location-tracking technologies but lacked comprehensive management features such as centralized data storage, automated report generation, real-time notifications, and cross-platform accessibility. Other systems focused mainly on student attendance and provided few communication and coordination tools for coordinators, supervisors, and partner companies.
Some existing  studies also lacked real-time synchronization across multiple devices across multiple devices such as web browsers and mobile phones. This limitation affects accessibility and  convenience, especially for students and coordinators who need immediate access to attendance records, progress reports, evaluation forms, and internship updates. Furthermore, many existing systems were developed for specific institutions and may not fully address the workflow and operational needs of Colegio de Montalban, particularly for the ICS and IBE departments.

Another gap that has been identified is the limited integration of intelligent attendance verification with centralized internship management processes. Although some systems successfully implemented automated attendance technologies, these features were not fully integrated with communication, evaluation, documentation, and monitoring functions within a single unified platform. As a result, internship management processes may still become inefficient and difficult to manage.
To address these gaps, the proposed Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System aims to integrate attendance monitoring, selfie-based verification, progress tracking, report submission, communication, internship hour computation, evaluation management, automated notifications, and centralized record management into one unified system accessible through web and mobile platforms. Unlike existing systems that focus only on selected functionalities, the proposed system aims to provide a more comprehensive and centralized internship management solution specifically designed for the operational needs of Colegio de Montalban.
The proposed study aims to improve real-time monitoring, accessibility, transparency, usability, and efficiency in managing OJT processes for students, coordinators, supervisors, and partner companies. Through the integration of verified attendance mechanisms and centralized monitoring technologies, the proposed system seeks to reduce administrative workload across all user roles, improve documentation accuracy, strengthen multi-platform communication, and optimize overall internship management processes for Colegio de Montalban.

CHAPTER 3: METHODOLOGY 
Research Design
 This study will use a developmental-descriptive research design with a mixed-methods approach to develop and evaluate the Cross-Platform On-the-Job Training Monitoring and Management System for Colegio de Montalban.
The developmental aspect of the study will focus on the planning, designing, developing, testing, implementing, and evaluating of the proposed system. The researchers will use the identified problems and requirements of the current manual OJT process as the basis for designing the system’s features, user interfaces, database, and workflows.
The descriptive aspect will be used to document and analyze the existing OJT monitoring process, including attendance recording, rendered-hour computation, report submission, document verification, progress monitoring, communication, and performance evaluation. This will allow the researchers to identify the limitations of the existing process and determine the requirements of the proposed system.
A mixed-methods approach will also be used. Quantitative data will be gathered through survey questionnaires and system-evaluation forms. Qualitative data will be obtained through interviews and observations involving students, OJT coordinators, company supervisors, and other qualified participants. The quantitative results will be summarized statistically, while the qualitative responses will be organized according to recurring issues, suggestions, and system requirements.
The researchers will use the Agile Software Development Life Cycle as the development methodology. Agile will allow the researchers to develop the system incrementally, test each feature, obtain feedback from intended users, and apply revisions throughout the development process.
ResearchLocale                                                       

The study will be conducted at Colegio de Montalban, Rodriguez, Rizal, particularly among the personnel, students, and partner-company representatives involved in the OJT implementation of the Institute of Computing Studies and Institute of Business and Entrepreneurship.
The institution was selected as the research locale because it currently manages OJT-related processes involving student applications, attendance monitoring, report submission, progress tracking, evaluation, and completion documentation. The existing processes and the experiences of the intended users will serve as the basis for identifying system requirements and evaluating the proposed solution

System / Project Overview 
The proposed Cross-Platform On-the-Job Training Monitoring and Management System is a centralized web and mobile application designed for the management and monitoring of OJT activities of fourth-year students under the ICS and IBE departments of Colegio de Montalban.
The system is intended to address problems related to manual attendance records, delayed report submission, scattered documents, difficulty in computing rendered hours, delayed communication, and limited visibility of student progress.
The system will provide role-based access for the following users:
Students, who will record attendance, submit reports and requirements, view rendered hours, and monitor their OJT progress.
OJT coordinators, who will review student records, validate attendance, check reports, monitor completion, and manage OJT-related activities.
Company supervisors, who will verify trainee attendance, provide feedback, and submit performance evaluations.
Program heads, who will monitor overall OJT implementation and review summarized student records.
System administrators, who will manage user accounts, system records, announcements, and configuration settings.
IT experts or qualified evaluators, who will assess the technical quality of the developed system.
The system will include attendance monitoring, selfie-based attendance verification, rendered-hour computation, report and document submission, progress monitoring, performance evaluation, notifications, announcements, and centralized record management.

According to Ramos et al. (2024), integrating face recognition and automated monitoring technologies improves attendance accuracy, usability, and accessibility in internship monitoring systems. The system was designed to operate across mobile and web platforms to ensure accessibility and convenience for students, coordinators, supervisors, and administrators. The main users of the system include: OJT Students OJT Coordinators Company Supervisors Program Heads Partner Companies The proposed system utilizes a centralized database architecture to ensure real-time synchronization and organized management of internship-related records.
