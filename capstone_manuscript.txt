ABSTRACT
This study aimed to design, develop, and evaluate a Cross-Platform On-the-Job Training (OJT) Monitoring and Management System for the Institute of Computing Studies (ICS) and the Institute of Business and Entrepreneurship (IBE) of Colegio de Montalban. The existing manual OJT process was found to be inefficient due to reliance on paper-based attendance logbooks, delayed report submissions, fragmented communication channels, and difficulty in tracking rendered hours and student progress.

The proposed system was developed using the Agile Software Development Life Cycle and operates across web, mobile, and desktop platforms. Core features include student identity proofing via validated school ID upload; a pre-deployment gateway requiring the Pre-OJT Orientation Certificate and Endorsement Letter; a Practicum Work Schedule Commitment engine enforcing CHED CMO No. 104, s. 2017 compliance; dual-biometric live selfie verification (Time In and Time Out) paired with modality-aware point-in-time GPS geotagging and supervisor verification; store-and-forward offline attendance synchronization via SQLite; supervisor-endorsed weekly report submission; real-time progress monitoring; structured two-tier performance evaluations; tamper-proof digital completion certificate verification; and a dedicated administrative desktop application via Electron. The system utilizes Next.js for the web application, React Native with Expo for the mobile application, Electron for the desktop application, and Supabase for cloud backend services.

The system was evaluated by selected students, OJT coordinators, company supervisors, and IT experts using selected characteristics of the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Product Quality Model, namely Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability, and Security (evaluating security-related features). Results of the evaluation demonstrate that the system adequately addresses the identified operational limitations of the manual process and provides an efficient, transparent, and legally compliant platform for managing OJT activities.

Keywords: On-the-Job Training, Cross-Platform System, Attendance Monitoring, Internship Management, CHED CMO 104, ISO/IEC 25010, Agile Methodology


CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background of the Study
In today’s rapidly evolving professional landscape, academic knowledge alone is no longer sufficient to ensure graduate employability. Employers increasingly seek individuals who can apply theoretical concepts in real-world workplace settings. On-the-Job Training (OJT) serves as a vital bridge between classroom instruction and professional practice. Globally, OJT and Work-Integrated Learning (WIL) programs are recognized for developing both technical competencies and essential soft skills such as communication, teamwork, adaptability, and time management (Garavan et al., as cited in Bouchrika, 2026; Deloitte, 2025).

Higher education institutions worldwide are progressively shifting from fragmented, paper-based internship management toward unified digital platforms. These systems function as central repositories for attendance logs, daily task reports, and performance assessments. They improve data integrity, reduce administrative overhead, and support compliance with institutional quality assurance standards.

In the Philippines, the Commission on Higher Education (CHED, 2021) mandates the inclusion of OJT in the curriculum of higher education programs. Despite this requirement, many institutions continue to rely on traditional manual processes. Students frequently encounter delayed feedback, unclear task assignments, and difficulty in monitoring their rendered hours. Coordinators face challenges related to paper logbooks, misplaced documents, inconsistent records, and time-consuming verification procedures. Partner companies often rely on general messaging applications that are not designed for structured internship management, resulting in scattered information and reduced transparency.

Previous local studies have attempted to address these challenges. Researchers from Bohol Island State University developed OJT AtTRACK, a face-recognition-based system that improved real-time monitoring and reduced attendance fraud (Abad et al., 2023). Other studies introduced management information systems that enhanced record tracking and reduced paperwork (Cabalbag & Ontiveros, 2024; Reyes & Dela Cruz, 2020). While these efforts demonstrate the value of digital solutions, most existing systems remain limited to a single platform or address only selected functions such as attendance or report submission.

At Colegio de Montalban, the OJT program for fourth-year students under the Institute of Computing Studies (ICS) and the Institute of Business and Entrepreneurship (IBE) is still managed through manual application forms, physical reports, paper logbooks, and decentralized communication channels. This situation creates operational inefficiencies for students, coordinators, and partner companies. In response, this study proposes the development of a Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System that integrates attendance verification, document management, progress tracking, performance evaluation, and stakeholder communication into a single accessible platform operating on web, mobile, and desktop environments.

1.2 Statement of the Problem
Colegio de Montalban currently manages the On-the-Job Training (OJT) program for fourth-year students of the Institute of Computing Studies (ICS) and the Institute of Business and Entrepreneurship (IBE) through fragmented and manual methods. Students experience delays in submitting requirements, tracking rendered hours, and communicating with coordinators. Faculty coordinators and company supervisors face inefficiencies caused by manual logbook verification, delayed physical submissions, and decentralized communication. These conditions result in inconsistent records, delayed progress monitoring, and increased risk of data errors.

This study therefore aims to design, develop, and evaluate a Cross-Platform On-the-Job Training Monitoring and Management System that provides centralized web, mobile, and desktop platforms for real-time attendance verification, document management, progress tracking, and structured communication among stakeholders.

Selected users participate in testing and provide feedback. 1.6 Scope and Limitations

Scope
This study focuses on the design, development, and evaluation of a Cross-Platform On-the-Job Training Monitoring and Management System for fourth-year students of the Institute of Computing Studies (ICS) and the Institute of Business and Entrepreneurship (IBE) at Colegio de Montalban.

The system supports the following user groups and functions:
• Students – Self-registration with mandatory school ID credential upload; pre-deployment credential submission (specifically limited to the Pre-OJT Orientation Certificate and Endorsement Letter); Practicum Work Schedule Commitment submission; dual-biometric attendance tracking (live selfie on Time In and Time Out) with modality-aware point-in-time GPS location verification; offline attendance storage via SQLite with automatic background synchronization; automated rendered-hour progress calculation; weekly accomplishment report submission; and supervisor evaluation review.
• OJT Coordinators and Program Heads – Validation of student registrations and ID credentials; pre-deployment gateway clearance; review of practicum schedule proposals; assignment of company supervisors; validation of attendance records; verification and grading of submitted weekly reports; real-time monitoring of student progress; and generation of departmental summary reports.
• Company Supervisors – Verification of trainee attendance with side-by-side Time In and Time Out selfie review; monitoring of daily activities; endorsement of weekly reports; and submission of midterm and final performance evaluations.
• System Administrators – Management of user accounts and roles; institutional announcements; system configuration; and administrative document archival via a dedicated desktop application developed using Electron.

Limitations
The study is limited to fourth-year BSIT students of the ICS and IBE departments of Colegio de Montalban. The system does not support job placement/recruitment, student hiring workflows, or the legal preparation of Memoranda of Agreement (MOA). Administrative tasks such as payroll computation and general human resource management are strictly excluded.

Technical limitations include:
1. Offline Functionality Boundary: Offline operation is strictly limited to mobile attendance logging with GPS satellite coordinate capture and local SQLite caching. All other modules—including account registration, document submission, schedule approval, evaluations, and administrative reporting—require an active internet connection.
2. Point-in-Time Proximity & Non-Lockout Boundary: Point-in-time GPS verification captures device coordinates strictly upon Time In and Time Out without continuous background tracking. While on-site trainees are evaluated against a 150-meter workplace proximity baseline, the system never prevents or blocks attendance submission. Trainees outside the perimeter (due to off-site errands, client fieldwork, or indoor GPS signal drift) provide an explanatory note and proceed to selfie capture, routing the log to the supervisor’s Smart Compliance Triage for human verification. Approved remote trainees are automatically tagged with a 'Remote Work' status.
3. Inactivity Session Boundary: Web portals enforce an automatic 30-minute inactivity session timeout to safeguard unattended institutional workstations in university computer laboratories.

1.8 Definition of Terms

Attendance Monitoring
Plain Meaning: The automated digital process of recording when a student starts and ends their daily internship work.
System Context: The mobile app records the exact date, time, GPS coordinates, and selfie photos of trainees during Time In and Time Out without relying on paper logbooks.

CHED CMO No. 104, Series of 2017 Compliance Engine
Plain Meaning: National government regulations issued by the Commission on Higher Education to protect student interns from being overworked or subjected to hazardous night shifts.
System Context: A built-in logic check that automatically rejects proposed internship schedules exceeding 8 hours per day or 40 hours per week, and restricts work hours strictly between 6:00 AM and 9:00 PM.

Dual-Biometric Selfie Verification
Plain Meaning: Taking a live photo of the trainee's face with the phone camera at two separate moments: upon arriving and upon leaving.
System Context: Requires students to take a live selfie at both Time In and Time Out, allowing the company supervisor to view both photos side-by-side to verify that the student did not leave immediately after logging in.

Electron Desktop Application
Plain Meaning: A technology that packages a web program so that it runs as an independent desktop software application on a computer, complete with its own desktop icon and window.
System Context: Provides system administrators with a dedicated desktop software program to manage accounts, broadcast announcements, and monitor records without opening a web browser.

Modality-Aware Point-in-Time GPS Geotagging
Plain Meaning: An instantaneous location check captured only when a student clocks in or out, providing proof of location without continuous monitoring or unfair lockouts.
System Context: Captures satellite coordinates at the moment of Time In and Time Out to verify proximity to the host establishment. If a trainee is deployed on an official off-site errand or working remotely, the system allows the entry with an activity note for supervisor review rather than blocking their attendance.

PostgreSQL Database
Plain Meaning: A secure digital filing cabinet that stores, organizes, and protects large volumes of institutional data.
System Context: The primary cloud database that permanently stores user accounts, student profiles, attendance logs, evaluation scores, and uploaded file references.

Pre-Deployment Documentary Gateway
Plain Meaning: A mandatory digital checkpoint that prevents students from advancing until their required paperwork is verified.
System Context: A system block that prevents students from selecting work schedules or logging attendance until the OJT Coordinator has inspected and approved both their Pre-OJT Orientation Certificate and Endorsement Letter.

Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
Plain Meaning: A digital security system that ensures users can only see and do what their specific role allows.
System Context: Restricts access so that students can only see their own attendance and reports, supervisors can only manage their assigned trainees, coordinators can review their department, and administrators can manage system accounts.

SQLite (Offline Local Database)
Plain Meaning: A miniature storage notebook built directly inside the mobile phone that operates without an internet connection.
System Context: Temporarily saves attendance records and selfie photos on the student's phone when cellular signal is unavailable, automatically sending them to the main database once the phone reconnects to the internet.

Supabase
Plain Meaning: A secure cloud platform that provides database storage, user account logins, and file hosting services in one unified package.
System Context: The backend service that manages secure user logins, stores uploaded student ID cards and selfies, and executes data security policies.

Tamper-Proof Digital Certificate Verification
Plain Meaning: A digital certificate with a unique security code that allows anyone to confirm that the certificate is genuine and has not been faked or altered.
System Context: Generates a unique verification code and public lookup page for each student upon completing their required OJT hours so that coordinators and employers can verify certificate authenticity.


CHAPTER 2
REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES

Digital and Automated Attendance Verification Systems
Facial recognition and related biometric technologies have become increasingly common in attendance monitoring systems because of their potential to improve accuracy, reduce fraud, and minimize manual intervention. Traditional attendance methods that rely on paper logbooks or simple digital checklists remain vulnerable to buddy punching, record tampering, and human error.

Abad et al. (2023) developed the OJT AtTRACK system, which utilized face recognition and cloud technology to automate attendance recording and compute rendered hours for OJT trainees. The system enabled coordinators to monitor trainees assigned to different locations in near real time and reduced opportunities for attendance fraud. Ramos et al. (2024) similarly integrated facial recognition into the SEAIT OJT Monitoring System to improve accessibility, security, and transparency while reducing reliance on manual checking.

Other studies have demonstrated the value of deep learning and computer vision techniques. Ahmed et al. (2023), Sharma et al. (2023), Seelam et al. (2021), Arsenovic et al. (2021), and Kumar et al. (2025) reported that AI-driven face recognition systems improve identity verification accuracy under varying environmental conditions and reduce administrative workload. These findings support the inclusion of selfie-based attendance verification in the proposed system.

Web-Based Internship and OJT Monitoring Systems
Web-based platforms have been widely used to centralize internship records and improve coordination among stakeholders. Dewi and Hanifah (2024) developed a web-based student internship monitoring information system that improved attendance monitoring, documentation, and remote supervision. Pratama et al. (2022) utilized the CodeIgniter framework to simplify trainee record management and report handling. Barrocan et al. (2025) and Jalaludin and Abu Seman (2023) further demonstrated that centralized web systems enhance accountability, report generation, and communication between trainees and supervisors.

Several web-based systems have also incorporated location-based verification. Systems that integrate GPS technologies help confirm that trainees are physically present at authorized internship sites before attendance is recorded, thereby strengthening the integrity of attendance data.

Mobile-Based and GPS-Enabled Monitoring Systems
Mobile technologies increase accessibility for students who may be deployed in different locations. Reyes et al. (2024) developed a mobile-based student internship monitoring system that incorporated a progress-tracking algorithm. The system allowed students, supervisors, and coordinators to access records and updates conveniently through smartphones. In earlier approaches, such as the Android-based Internship Attendance and Logbook Application (2024), geofencing technologies were used to verify trainee location before attendance could be recorded. However, while effective in fixed-location environments, such rigid geofencing models presented notable operational limitations when applied to dynamic corporate settings involving external client tasks, off-site errands, and indoor GPS satellite attenuation.

Internship Management Information Systems
Centralized management information systems improve the organization, accessibility, and evaluation of internship data. Cruz et al. (2023) developed a management information system for tracking on-the-job trainees that improved monitoring of performance, attendance, and requirements. Alshammari et al. (2025), Safdari and Ehtesham (2025), and Hendrawan et al. (2025) emphasized the value of integrated platforms that combine communication, monitoring, evaluation, and cloud-based access. These studies collectively support the development of a comprehensive, centralized system rather than fragmented single-function tools.

Synthesis of Related Literature
The reviewed literature confirms that manual, paper-based OJT monitoring is vulnerable to buddy-punching, lost records, and delayed communications. Previous research has explored biometric facial recognition, GPS tracking, and cloud-based management systems to address these problems (Abad et al., 2023; Ramos et al., 2024; Barrocan et al., 2025).

However, commercial human-resource tools and existing academic prototypes fail to address the specific regulatory and operational realities of tertiary OJT in the Philippines:
1. Lack of CHED Regulatory Enforcement: Standard attendance tools allow arbitrary overtime and night shifts. They lack automated compliance checks based on CHED CMO No. 104, s. 2017, which limits undergraduate internship hours to 8.0 hours per day, 40.0 hours per week, and prohibits night shifts between 9:00 PM and 6:00 AM.
2. Single-Check Biometric Loopholes: Most biometric solutions capture a photo only during morning clock-in, allowing trainees to leave immediately afterward. A dual-biometric model (Time In and Time Out) is necessary to ensure trainees remain on duty throughout their shift.
3. Invasive and Rigid Geofencing: Commercial tools and academic prototypes frequently employ continuous background location tracking and hard geofences that lock trainees out when satellite signals drift indoors or when assigned to legitimate off-site errands. This treats students like monitored inmates rather than young professionals. Modern internship management requires Point-in-Time GPS Geotagging with Supervisor Discretion, which respects student privacy outside the clock-in moment, eliminates false lockouts, and empowers supervisors to approve authorized fieldwork.
4. Lack of Gateway Prerequisite Checks: Most systems allow immediate attendance logging without verifying whether the student has completed pre-deployment requirements (e.g., orientation and institutional endorsement).

Research Gap
Existing solutions remain fragmented, focusing on isolated functions or single operating systems. At Colegio de Montalban, no unified platform currently connects students, faculty coordinators, company supervisors, and administrators across web, mobile, and desktop environments.

The proposed Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System fills this gap by combining student identity verification at registration, a two-document pre-deployment gateway, CHED CMO 104 schedule validation, dual-biometric mobile attendance logging with modality-aware point-in-time GPS geotagging, offline SQLite synchronization, supervisor-endorsed weekly reporting, and desktop administrative oversight into a unified system tailored specifically for the ICS and IBE departments.

METHODOLOGY

3.1 Research Design
This study employs a developmental-descriptive research design with a mixed-methods approach. The developmental component focuses on the systematic planning, design, development, testing, and evaluation of the Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System. The descriptive component is used to document and analyze the existing manual OJT monitoring processes, including attendance recording, rendered-hour computation, report submission, document verification, progress monitoring, communication, and performance evaluation.

Quantitative data are gathered through structured survey questionnaires and system evaluation forms. Qualitative data are obtained through semi-structured interviews and direct observation. The combination of quantitative and qualitative methods allows the researchers to obtain both measurable evaluations and in-depth insights into user needs and system performance.

The system is developed following the Agile Software Development Life Cycle, which supports iterative development, continuous user feedback, and progressive refinement of features.

3.2 Research Locale
The study is conducted at Colegio de Montalban, located in Rodriguez, Rizal. The research focuses on the On-the-Job Training programs implemented by the Institute of Computing Studies (ICS) and the Institute of Business and Entrepreneurship (IBE). The institution was selected because it currently manages OJT processes through manual methods and therefore provides a suitable context for identifying requirements and evaluating a digital solution.

3.3 System Overview
The Cross-Platform OJT Monitoring and Management System is a centralized application designed to operate across web browsers, mobile devices, and desktop environments. It provides role-based access for five distinct user groups: Students, OJT Coordinators, Company Supervisors, Program Heads, and System Administrators.

The core operational modules of the system include:
1. Student Identity Proofing: During mobile registration, students submit their institutional details along with a scanned image or photograph of their front-and-back Validated Student ID Card. Student accounts remain in a restricted pending state until verified by the OJT Coordinator, preventing unauthorized access.
2. Pre-Deployment Documentary Gateway: Before internship deployment, students must submit two mandatory institutional credentials: (a) the Pre-OJT Orientation Certificate and (b) the Official Endorsement Letter. The system blocks schedule proposal submission and attendance logging until both documents are approved by the coordinator.
3. Practicum Work Schedule Commitment (CHED CMO 104 Engine): Following gateway clearance, students submit their work schedule proposal, specifying their work modality (On-site, Hybrid, or Remote), weekly workdays (Monday through Saturday; Sundays prohibited), and daily shift times. The system enforces CHED CMO No. 104, s. 2017 rules (capping shifts at 8.0 hours/day and 40.0 hours/week between 6:00 AM and 9:00 PM). Upon approval, the coordinator assigns the student to an accredited company supervisor.
4. Dual-Biometric Attendance & Point-in-Time GPS Verification: Trainees log attendance using their smartphones with live camera selfie capture and point-in-time GPS coordinate recording at both Time In and Time Out. Proximity to the company office is evaluated without lockout: in-bounds entries are tagged as clean logs, while off-site entries allow students to submit an activity note for supervisor verification. Approved remote trainees are automatically exempted and tagged with a 'Remote Work' status.
5. Offline SQLite Store-and-Forward Engine: If a trainee logs attendance in an area without internet coverage, the record and encrypted selfie data are saved locally in SQLite and automatically synchronized once an internet connection is re-established.
6. Supervisor Endorsement and Progress Monitoring: Company supervisors review attendance records with side-by-side Time In and Time Out selfies, endorse weekly accomplishment reports, and submit midterm and final evaluations based on a 5-dimension Likert rubric.
7. Administrative Desktop Runtime: System administrators utilize a dedicated Electron desktop application that provides native window controls, system tray minimization, user management, and read-only archival reporting.

The system architecture utilizes Next.js (App Router) for the web portal, React Native with Expo for the mobile application, Electron for the desktop client, and Supabase (PostgreSQL, Supabase Auth, Storage, and Row-Level Security) for cloud backend services.

3.5 Sampling Technique
The study utilizes purposive sampling. This technique is appropriate because the researchers specifically require participants who possess direct experience with or knowledge of the current OJT monitoring and management processes at Colegio de Montalban (Etikan et al., 2016). Students are selected based on their enrollment in the OJT program. Coordinators, program heads, and company supervisors are selected based on their official responsibilities related to internship supervision and evaluation. IT experts are selected based on their technical background and ability to assess software quality.

3.6 Research Instruments
The following instruments are used to gather the necessary data:

3.6.1 Survey Questionnaire
A structured survey questionnaire is administered to gather information about the current OJT process, problems encountered by users, and desired system features. The questionnaire contains sections on respondent profile, current practices, problems in attendance and hour computation, report submission, communication and evaluation, and recommended features.

3.6.2 Interview Guide
A semi-structured interview guide is used to obtain qualitative insights from OJT coordinators, selected students, and company supervisors. The guide covers existing workflows, attendance validation procedures, report verification, communication practices, performance evaluation methods, and recommendations for system improvement.

3.6.3 Observation Checklist
An observation checklist is used to document actual practices related to attendance handling, report submission, record management, and communication methods under the existing manual system.

3.6.4 System Evaluation Questionnaire
A system evaluation questionnaire based on selected characteristics of the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Product Quality Model is administered after the system prototype or completed version is demonstrated. The questionnaire measures Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability, and Security. Each characteristic contains specific statements rated on a five-point Likert scale.

Sample Evaluation Statements:
Functional Suitability
• The system provides complete functions needed for OJT attendance monitoring and report management.
• The system correctly computes rendered internship hours in compliance with approved schedules.
• The system appropriately supports the roles of students, coordinators, and supervisors.

Performance Efficiency
• The system responds to user actions within an acceptable time.
• Data are synchronized properly across web and mobile platforms upon network reconnection.

Usability
• The user interface is clear and easy to understand.
• Users can complete common tasks without difficulty.
• The system is easy to learn for first-time users.

Security (Security-related features were evaluated)
In accordance with the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 product quality standard, security-related features were evaluated across the following operational areas:
• The system enforces secure role-based access control (RBAC) via Next.js middleware, preventing unauthorized URL access prior to page rendering.
• The system implements an automatic 30-minute inactivity timeout across administrative and coordinator web portals to secure unattended workstations in shared university computer laboratories.
• Sensitive student documents and attendance photos are stored in private storage buckets, accessible only through time-limited signed URLs.
• Database operations are protected by PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS) policies, ensuring users can access only tenancy-authorized records.
• Critical authentication events—including failed logins, access attempts on pending accounts, and successful authentications—are recorded in an immutable security audit log.

3.7 Validation of Instruments
Prior to data collection, the survey questionnaire, interview guide, observation checklist, and system evaluation questionnaire are submitted to qualified validators. The validators include an OJT coordinator or program representative, a research methodology adviser, and an IT instructor or software development expert. The validators examine the instruments for clarity, relevance, completeness, and alignment with the research objectives. Their recommendations are incorporated into the final versions of the instruments.

3.8 Data Gathering Procedure
The researchers follow these steps:
1. Secure necessary permission from the appropriate school authorities and department heads.
2. Validate all research instruments with qualified experts.
3. Identify and invite qualified respondents according to the inclusion criteria.
4. Obtain informed consent from all participants.
5. Administer the survey questionnaire through Google Forms or printed forms.
6. Conduct semi-structured interviews with selected participants.
7. Perform observations of existing OJT processes, subject to institutional permission.
8. Demonstrate the system prototype or completed version to evaluators.
9. Administer the system evaluation questionnaire.
10. Organize, store, and analyze the collected data.

3.9 Data Analysis Procedure
Quantitative data from the survey and system evaluation questionnaires are analyzed using descriptive statistics. Frequency and percentage are used to summarize respondent profiles and checklist responses. The weighted mean is used to determine the average rating for each evaluation criterion.

The weighted mean is computed using the formula:
WM = Σ(fx) / N
where:
• WM = weighted mean
• f = frequency of each rating
• x = numerical value of the rating
• N = total number of responses

Qualitative data from interviews and observations are analyzed through thematic analysis. Responses are organized according to recurring problems, requirements, suggestions, and implementation concerns.

3.10 System Development Methodology
The system is developed using the Agile Software Development Life Cycle. The process is organized into the following iterative stages:

Stage 1: Planning and Requirements Analysis – Examination of the existing OJT process through surveys, interviews, and observations to identify user roles, problems, required features, and technical constraints.
Stage 2: System and Interface Design – Preparation of system architecture, database design, user interface mockups, navigation flows, use-case diagrams, and prototypes for web, mobile, and desktop versions.
Stage 3: Iterative Development – Implementation of features across 9 distinct Agile sprints:
• Sprint 1: Authentication, Role Management, and Identity Proofing. Implementation of role-based registration, front-and-back student ID card upload, coordinator verification queue, route middleware access controls, and 30-minute laboratory session inactivity monitoring.
• Sprint 2: Pre-Deployment Gateway and Practicum Schedule Engine. Implementation of the two-document prerequisite gateway (Pre-OJT Orientation Certificate and Endorsement Letter), Practicum Work Schedule Commitment submission, and automated CHED CMO No. 104, s. 2017 compliance checks.
• Sprint 3: Dual-Biometric Attendance and Offline Synchronization. Development of mobile attendance tracking featuring mandatory live selfie capture on both Time In and Time Out, modality-aware point-in-time GPS geotagging (with 150-meter proximity baseline, off-site errand exception notes, and remote work exemption), and local SQLite offline queueing.
• Sprint 4: Rendered-Hour Computation. Development of the automated hour calculation engine, which deducts scheduled lunch breaks and caps credit hours to the approved daily maximum to prevent unapproved overtime.
• Sprint 5: Document and Weekly Report Submission. Implementation of weekly narrative accomplishment report submissions, supporting PDF attachments, and company supervisor review and endorsement.
• Sprint 6: Progress Monitoring. Implementation of real-time hour progress tracking, requirement completion indicators, and department-level dashboard overviews for OJT Coordinators and Program Heads.
• Sprint 7: Performance Evaluation. Creation of structured midterm and final evaluation forms using a 5-dimension Likert rubric (Technical Competence, Work Quality, Professionalism, Punctuality, Communication) with automatic percentage grade computation.
• Sprint 8: Notifications and Announcements. Integration of centralized in-app notifications and administrative bulletin announcements.
• Sprint 9: Administrative Desktop Client and Certificate Archival. Development of the Electron desktop administrative wrapper, system tray integration, read-only completed record archival, and tamper-proof digital completion certificate generation.
Stage 4: Testing – Conduct of functional testing, usability testing, compatibility testing across supported browsers and devices, and security-related checks (authentication, role-based access, and protection of uploaded files).
Stage 5: User Feedback and Revision – Collection of feedback from selected users and incorporation of necessary improvements.
Stage 6: Deployment and Final Review – Preparation of the completed system for demonstration or pilot use and assessment of whether the system addresses the problems identified in the current manual process.

3.11 Hardware and Software Requirements

Hardware Requirements
1. Development & Evaluation Computer: Intel Core i5 or AMD Ryzen 5 processor, 8 GB RAM, and 256 GB SSD (verified to compile Next.js web components, build React Native mobile bundles, and execute the Electron desktop runtime smoothly).
2. Mobile Test Devices: Android (version 10.0 or higher) and iOS smartphones equipped with functional GPS satellite receivers and front-facing cameras for selfie verification and location proximity testing.
3. Network Environment: Minimum 5 Mbps internet connection for cloud synchronization; cellular data or offline simulation environments for offline queue validation.

Software Requirements
1. Visual Studio Code – Source code development and debugging.
2. Next.js (Version 14, App Router) – React-based web framework for coordinator, supervisor, program head, and admin portals.
3. React Native & Expo (Expo Go) – Cross-platform framework for the student mobile application.
4. Electron.js (Version 28+) – Desktop application runtime for the administrative management console.
5. TypeScript – Strongly typed language for web, mobile, and shared modules.
6. Tailwind CSS & NativeWind – Utility-first interface styling for web and mobile components.
7. Supabase – Cloud platform providing PostgreSQL database, Supabase Auth, private file storage, and Row-Level Security (RLS).
8. SQLite – Embedded client-side relational database for offline mobile attendance caching.
9. Git & GitHub – Source code version control and collaborative development repository.
